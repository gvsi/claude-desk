## 1. Meta Muse / Muse for Small Business (Sept 2026)

### What it is (dates)
- **Muse** (personal agent): announced **8 Sep 2026**, updated 30 Sep ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)). US and Canada. Free with usage limits, plus paid tiers ($20 "Power" and $100 "Maximum" per a Meta help page, [subscriptions help](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/)).
- **Muse for Small Business**: announced **29 Sep 2026**, updated 8 Oct ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-small-business/); [CNBC, 29 Sep](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/meta-launches-muse-for-small-business-zuckerberg-pushes-enterprise-ai.html)). It is the same app with business skills and connectors added: FB/IG business accounts, IG analytics, Pages, Meta ad accounts, plus Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe and Zoom. It also supports custom connectors.
- **Important difference from Claude Desk:** Muse for Small Business works *for the owner*, not *in front of customers*. Neither Meta's post nor the third-party coverage I found describes it answering a business's customers (CellCog, secondary: "doesn't describe any features that interact directly with a business's customers", [29 Sep](https://cellcog.ai/blog/muse-for-small-business/)). Its UX patterns therefore carry over to the **owner console**. They do not carry over to the public page.

### Onboarding
- The promise: "Anyone can use it out of the box, no technical experience required", and "there's no learning curve" ([Meta, 8 Sep](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)). For SMB: Muse "will start out already understanding your business" once tools are connected ([Meta, 29 Sep](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-small-business/)).
- The flow, per a hands-on account (MindStudio, secondary, [12 Sep](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/meta-muse-ai-agent)): it asks your name and lets you **name the agent**. FB/IG were already connected, and it **built a profile from them before suggesting how it could help**. It then asked for Gmail and calendar and came back with a summary of your "weekly rhythm". Setup took "a handful of conversational exchanges" rather than a settings menu. The app looks like a messaging app, with a main chat plus a proactive-suggestions feed, goals, an **activity log**, an **approvals queue**, **upcoming** tasks, and an identity/memory area.
- The Neuron's guide (secondary, [9 Sep](https://theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/how-to-get-started-with-meta-muse/)): **link one service first, tied to your first task**. Give a bounded task that says what you want to approve, and review the result before expanding. Signup asks for a card even on the free tier (citing TechCrunch). Training on interaction data is **on by default**, and you can opt out.
- Meta's getting-started help: type a request, review, ask for changes. "Muse may ask clarifying questions first" ([help](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1331373868832401/)).

### How it asks permission
- The headline promise for SMB: "**nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval.** You're in control." ([Meta, 29 Sep](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-small-business/)).
- **Two global modes** under Settings > Permissions: "**Ask for some actions**" (before every write action and important read actions) or "**Always ask**". Web access has the same two modes, plus an allowed-sites list you can revoke. The help page is undated ("7 weeks ago" at fetch) ([help: guidance and approval](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1385290430137537/)).
- **The approval ladder** in each prompt: *Allow once / Allow for this task / Allow for this site / Always allow (for the Connector) / Deny*, with "**See task details**" before deciding (same page). A third-party write-up lists five scopes, "Once, Session, Task, Time-bound, Permanent". That list does not match Meta's help page, so treat it as **UNVERIFIED** ([SmartScope, 25 Oct](https://smartscope.blog/en/blog/meta-muse-checking-execution-history-approvals-stopping-unintended-2026/)).
- **Read and write are split per connector**: "Many Connectors can also be set up so that Muse is only able to retrieve data, but not take actions" ([help: connectors](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1687253048996149/)). For Shopify, write access needs a separate approval in Shopify admin, and some actions (refund, cancel, edit orders) are simply not exposed (CellCog, secondary).
- **Some actions always ask**: Muse "will always ask for your approval prior to completing a purchase", using Link by Stripe one-time card numbers ([help: payments](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1436362127544482/)).
- **Checks are enforced outside the model**: "Many of these approval checks are enforced outside the AI model". The credential store lets Muse act "without the AI model seeing your password". Before credentials are used, code checks the target service, that the user authorised it, and that the action type is permitted ([help: privacy, safety and security](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1047255454427887/)). A separate **Sentinel** agent runs "kept apart from Muse at the system level". "Nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless the Sentinel approves it" ([Meta, 8 Sep](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)).
- **Risky detail**: "Muse learns over time which decisions need your approval" (help: guidance and approval). The model therefore widens its own autonomy. This conflicts with the "only narrows automatically" principle in our safety research.
- **Undo is honest**: you can ask Muse to stop some actions, but "actions like sending an email can't be reversed". Disconnecting a connector stops future actions. Reset deletes everything (help: guidance and approval).

### Presence, tone, and why it feels simple
- **Live status avatar** at the top ("Is working"). Tap it for the **Activity log** (actions *and permissions granted*) and **Upcoming** (help: guidance and approval). "Muse shows people a complete audit trail of everything it has done **and plans to do**" (Meta, 8 Sep).
- Talking to it "works just like messaging another person" (Meta, 8 Sep). It comes back "when something changes or when it needs approval", and approvals can come through the app or **WhatsApp** (The Neuron).
- Memory is visible and editable: you "can always tell it to 'forget' specific things".
- What makes it feel simple: (1) **one sentence of promise** (publish/send/spend); (2) **two modes**, not a matrix; (3) **setup by conversation over data it already has**; (4) a **single stream** with a queue of decisions; (5) **progressive trust**, where you start with a low-risk task.

### Reception
- Positive: one journalist called it "a really good tool" after several days' use (The Neuron). Alex Volkov called it "the most polished AI employee yet?" and said he was "genuinely impressed" (via [HackerNoon roundup](https://hackernoon.com/what-100-tech-people-think-metas-muse-is), secondary). An SMB owner said it audited her email automations and rebuilt templates ([Coverager](https://coverager.com/meta-expands-muse-to-small-businesses/), secondary).
- Negative: The Verge reportedly found it works but felt uneasy about how it operates (secondhand via a Chinese roundup, **UNVERIFIED**). Forbes framed it as asking people to hand over "their inbox, their calendar and their credit card" (via HackerNoon, secondary). Meta says prompt injection is unresolved and that Muse will make mistakes. Meta can access data to operate the service, and a private "Confidential VM" is planned (The Neuron).

### What Claude Desk should do
1. **Copy the one-sentence promise**, rewritten for a public agent: *"Claude only does what your rules allow. Anything else waits for you."* Put it in setup, Rules and the public page footer.
2. **Setup by conversation over data you already have.** After explicit permission, read Claude memories and the website, then show a "Here's what I understand about Turner Plumbing" card that the owner corrects. The first connection should be tied to the first use case (booking means Calendar first).
3. **Two global modes, not a matrix**: *Ask me for anything that commits the business* (default) / *Ask me for everything*. Per-tool detail lives one level down.
4. **An approval card with a scoped ladder**, adapted to strangers: *Allow once · Always for this customer · Always for requests like this (up to $X) · Deny · Deny and block*, plus "See details". Drop "for this site". Add expiry to the "always" options.
5. **Some actions are always ask, shown with a lock**: payments above a cap, identity or contact changes, and document release to anyone who is not on record. The owner cannot switch these to automatic.
6. **Do not copy "learns over time which decisions need your approval".** Claude Desk may *suggest* widening ("You approved 5 of these, make it automatic?"), but only an owner tap can widen it.
7. **Presence plus a single decision queue**: a live status line ("Talking with 2 people · 1 needs you") opens to Activity (done and planned) and Upcoming. Approvals reach the phone and SMS, as Muse uses WhatsApp.
8. **Be honest about irreversibility** in every approval card ("Can't be undone once sent").

## 2. Mid-conversation identity verification

### Standards baseline: NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, Aug 2025; [pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))
- **Email is not an out-of-band authenticator.** §3.1.3.1: "Email SHALL NOT be used for out-of-band authentication". Risks named: access with only a password, interception, and DNS rerouting. Email *confirmation codes* and *recovery codes* are a separate category and are allowed (§4.2).
- **SMS/voice is "restricted"**. §3.1.3.3 and §3.2.9: verifiers "SHALL ensure that alternative authenticator types are available", "Provide subscribers with meaningful notice" of the risks, and SHOULD check risk indicators (device swap, SIM change, porting) before sending. *Setting or changing the pre-registered number counts as binding a new authenticator* and follows the binding rules (§4.1.2). In other words, a number cannot be swapped in-band.
- **Secret parameters**: at least 6 digits; valid for **10 minutes**; accepted **once**; rate-limited, and a new code does **not** reset the failure count. At most 100 consecutive failures, and lower limits are allowed (§3.1.3.2, §3.2.2).
- **Recovery** (§4.2.2): issued recovery codes of at least 6 digits expire in **10 min by SMS/voice and 24 h by email**. A recovery address exists only after the subscriber confirms a code. §4.2.5: "account recovery SHALL cause a notification to be sent to the subscriber". §4.6: notifications must include clear instructions and contact information for disputing the event.
- **Usability** (§8, informative): give users ~2 minutes for codes. "Allow at least 10 entry attempts". Offer to show the digits during entry. Support copy/paste on mobile.

**Reading for Claude Desk:** an email or SMS code is a reasonable **low-to-moderate assurance** proof that someone "controls the contact on file". It is not strong authentication. That fits order status, rebooking or a receipt. For high-value release (a lease, a refund to a new method) it should be combined with **owner approval** or a stronger factor.

### The 2026 Meta support-assistant incident (the fraud lesson)
- Meta's AI "High Touch Support" recovery assistant launched in March 2026. Attackers used a VPN to appear near the target and asked the assistant to "link the account to an email address under their control". Because of "a bug in a separate code path", the system "did not properly verify that the email address provided" matched the account. It "incorrectly sent a password reset link to that unassociated email rather than rejecting the request". **20,225 accounts** were taken over where the owner had no 2FA. The first access is dated **17 Apr** in a Maine AG filing, and Meta found the bug on **31 May**. Meta disabled the tool, invalidated the links and is reviewing "similar account recovery flows across Meta's platforms" ([Help Net Security, 8 Jun 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/08/instagram-ai-support-vulnerability-account-takeovers/); [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/meta-ai-support-data-breach-affects-20-000-instagram-accounts)). Engadget reported that 2FA accounts were also at risk, which conflicts with Meta's account (**UNVERIFIED**).
- Lessons: (1) the model "worked as intended", and the failure was a **missing deterministic check on one path**. (2) Location was used as a trust signal and was spoofed. (3) **Never send a credential or code to a contact typed in the chat.** (4) The breach ran for six weeks before anyone noticed, so **notify the contact on record** on every verification and release, so the real person sees abuse early. (5) Victims had no route to a human.

### Production patterns
- **Intercom**: "Verify email with OTP" workflow step with built-in success/failure branches (**20 Feb 2026**, [changelog](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/127446-email-otp-verification-step-now-available-in-workflows)). "Verify phone with OTP" on calls (**3 Jul 2026**): "Callers on a landline who can't receive a text are verified by email instead, on the same call". Unverified callers go to voicemail or a follow-up path ([changelog](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/150513-verify-a-caller-s-identity-over-the-phone)). A community answer describes a ~30-minute verified window before connector calls (secondary). For the logged-in web case, Messenger identity now uses JWT, and the old HMAC identity verification is deprecated ([Fin help](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13975780-what-is-identity-verification-deprecated)).
- **Genesys Cloud**: step-up mid-chat, where "agent can ask the customer to log in and continue the conversation in an authenticated manner". **History carries over** ([help](https://help.genesys.cloud/articles/step-up-authentication-during-a-web-messaging-conversation/)).
- **Fin's guidance on AI identity checks** ([fin.ai learn](https://fin.ai/learn/customer-identity-verification-ai-agents), undated 2026): tier verification by action risk. Send codes to "a pre-registered phone number or verified email, not to whatever channel the current conversation uses". On failure, "a clean handoff to a human with full context", not endless retries. **Generic failure messages**, because specific ones reveal whether an account exists. Make verification "a workflow gate the model cannot skip".
- **OAuth step-up** ([RFC 9470](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc9470/)): the resource returns `insufficient_user_authentication` with `acr_values`/`max_age`, which matches "needs verification, verified within 15 minutes".

### Magic link or code inside a chat
- Email security scanners (for example Microsoft Safe Links) **open links to inspect them and consume one-time tokens**, so users see "invalid or expired" ([Stytch docs](https://stytch.com/docs/consumer-auth/authentication/magic-links/overview.md); [Supabase troubleshooting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/otp-verification-failures-token-has-expired-or-otp_expired-errors-5ee4d0)). A link also opens a **new tab or device** and can strand the chat. **Default to a 6-digit code typed into the chat.** If a link is used, it should open a confirmation page with a button (no token use on GET) and show the code as a fallback.

### Fraud: SMS pumping
- Bots trigger OTP sends to premium-rate numbers. Twilio Verify **Fraud Guard** blocks suspicious prefixes, and Twilio claims "$62.7M saved" (vendor figures vary, [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/twilio-verify-fraud-guard-a-powerful-defense-against-sms-pumping-fraud)). **Sending only to numbers already on the owner's record removes most of this attack surface**, because the attacker cannot choose the destination. Still rate-limit sends per session, IP and record, and cap daily sends per Desk.

### Accessibility
- WCAG 2.2 **SC 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), AA**: no cognitive-function test unless a mechanism assists. For codes this means **paste works** and **autofill works**: use a single field with `autocomplete="one-time-code"` and `inputmode="numeric"`. Segmented 6-box inputs often break paste and autofill ([W3C Understanding](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/accessible-authentication.html); [Auth0](https://auth0.com/blog/an-accessible-guide-to-wcag-3-3-8-authentication-without-frustration/)). Add an `aria-live` announcement ("Code sent to the phone ending 42"), a visible countdown with ≥2 minutes, show the digits by default (NIST §8), and offer a voice-call or email alternative (NIST §3.2.9, Intercom landline pattern).

### What Claude Desk should do
1. **The gate triggers verification, not the model.** When a tool needs a higher tier, the code gate returns `needs_verification(level, max_age)`. Claude then says plainly why: *"To show your order, I need to confirm it's you. I'll text a code to the number Turner has on file for you, ending ••42."*
2. **Known-party codes go only to the contact on record, shown masked.** The visitor picks *which* contact on file to use (phone ••42 or email j•••@g•••.com), never a new one. If they type a different number or email, that does not become a verification target. It becomes a lead note and an owner callback.
3. **Keep the conversation going while verification is pending.** An inline card (code field, Resend in 30 s, "Use email instead", "I can't access this") sits in the thread. The visitor can keep asking public questions. Once verified, the original request resumes automatically with a receipt such as "Verified · phone ••42 · 2:14 pm".
4. **No record, or the visitor can't verify**: say so neutrally ("I can't open account details here, but I can take a message and Turner will call you back"), collect name and contact as a lead, and route the request to the owner with full context. Never reveal whether a record exists.
5. **Code parameters**: 6 digits, 10 minutes, single use, 10 attempts per verification (failures persist across resends), at most 3 sends per session, and session verification valid for 15–30 min, scoped to that visitor's records. SMS text names the business and the action, plus a warning: *"Turner Plumbing: 482913 confirms it's you to view your booking. Turner will never call to ask for this code."*
6. **Notify the contact on record after every verified release** (NIST §4.2.5 analogue): "Your lease was shared after a code to ••42 at 2:14 pm. Not you? Reply STOP-SHARE / contact Turner."
7. **One verification service, tested on every entry path** (web chat, embed, visiting agents, owner takeover). This addresses the Meta "separate code path" failure. The owner's Safety screen lists each entry point with its verification status.
8. **Visiting agents**: an agent cannot receive the code, so the code goes to the human principal's contact on record. The human may relay it to their agent. The SMS says which agent asked ("requested via an AI assistant").
9. **Accessibility**: one field with autocomplete, paste and shown digits, a voice-call alternative, and screen-reader announcements.

## 3. MCP tool profiling and safety

### Annotation status (as of Oct 2026)
- Annotations shipped in spec **2025-03-26** and are still in the current spec. The four hints are `readOnlyHint`, `destructiveHint`, `idempotentHint` and `openWorldHint`, plus display `title`. **Defaults are pessimistic**: "a tool with no annotations is assumed to be non-read-only, potentially destructive, non-idempotent, and open-world" ([MCP blog, 16 Mar 2026](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-03-16-tool-annotations/)).
- The normative trust rule in the current draft tools page reads "clients **MUST** consider tool annotations to be untrusted unless they come from trusted servers" ([spec: tools, draft](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/server/tools)). The blog adds: "An untrusted server can lie", and annotations "aren't enforcement… that's a job for network controls or sandboxing".
- Spec **2026-07-28** (latest release) made the core stateless and added Extensions, Tasks, MCP Apps and multi-round-trip `input_required` results ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/); details via secondary sources). Relevant here: `tools/list` "**MAY** vary by the authorization presented on the request". A server can therefore expose fewer tools to a less-privileged caller. The list **MUST NOT** vary as a side effect of other requests. `notifications/tools/list_changed` signals changes.
- **Proposals in progress**: the Tool Annotations Interest Group was chartered **20 Apr 2026** and is facilitated by GitHub and OpenAI. Draft SEPs: **1862** Tool Resolution (preflight), **1913** Trust and Sensitivity Annotations, **1984** Comprehensive Tool Annotations, **2417** Model Preferences. Also proposed: 1487 `trustedHint`, 1560 `secretHint`, 1561 `unsafeOutputHint` ([charter](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/tool-annotations/charter)). None of these is in the spec yet.
- **Ecosystem**: Anthropic's connector directory **requires** "a title and the applicable readOnlyHint or destructiveHint" on every tool ([submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)). OpenAI's app submission reportedly requires all three of read-only, open-world and destructive to be set explicitly ([community thread, May 2026](https://community.openai.com/t/mcp-submission-still-blocked-annotations-error-despite-correct-manifest-may-2026/1381645), secondary). The MCP blog notes "no MCP client lets users filter tools by annotation values" and that most clients "treat installation itself as the trust signal".

### Tool poisoning and "rug pulls"
- **Tool poisoning** (Invariant Labs, **1 Apr 2025**, [blog](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-security-notification-tool-poisoning-attacks)): instructions are hidden in tool descriptions that the model reads but the user never sees. Variants are exfiltration of local files and **tool shadowing**, where one server rewrites how another server's tool is used, for example by redirecting email recipients. **Rug pull**: "a malicious server can change the tool description after the client has already approved it."
- Invariant's mitigations: show full descriptions and separate user-visible from model-visible text; **pin tools and packages by hash or checksum**; enforce cross-server dataflow boundaries. OWASP files this as **MCP03:2025 Tool Poisoning** ([OWASP MCP Top 10](https://owasp.org/www-project-mcp-top-10/)). Common practice (secondary): hash name, description and inputSchema at approval, compare on every reconnect, **block and alert on change** rather than updating silently, and treat servers like dependencies. `mcp-scan` pins tool hashes and alerts on changes (secondary; repo not checked). Pinning does not help against a server that is malicious from the first day.

### How Claude presents per-tool permissions
- **claude.ai connectors**: "For each permission category or individual permission, select **Always allow, Needs approval, or Blocked**". Tools are "categorized by type (for example, read-only tools, write/delete tools)". On Team/Enterprise plans this is "org-wide… individual users can't override it". "Restricting actions in Claude never grants more access than the source system permits" ([support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-use-connectors-to-extend-claude-s-capabilities)). Enterprise roles default to **Needs approval** on every connector. The stricter of org policy and role wins. A member whose tool is capped at Needs approval sees only "Ask" / "Never". Some write tools cannot be set to Always allow at all (Microsoft 365 `outlook_send_mail`). In Cowork, "Always allow" for write tools is **off by default** (support articles via search results; [M365 guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12684923-microsoft-365-connector-security-guide)). That the read/write grouping comes from annotations is my inference, **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Claude Managed Agents** ([docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/permission-policies), beta `managed-agents-2026-04-01`): `always_allow` / `always_ask` / `auto` per toolset and per tool. **MCP toolsets default to `always_ask`** "so that new tools added to an MCP server do not run in your application without approval". `auto` evaluates each call and returns allow, ask or deny. Denials cannot be overridden by the client. The docs warn: "**`auto` is not a human checkpoint**". They also note that end-user text relayed as `user.message` counts as intent, so "Configure `always_ask` on the tools you would not let that end user run without review". Every tool event records `evaluated_permission` and `evaluation.reason_code`, which works as an audit trail of the decision. Running sessions keep the tool configuration they were created with.
- A reported gap: Claude Code CLI did not honour claude.ai per-tool "approval required" settings ([issue](https://claudeissues.com/issue/64521-claude-code-cli-does-not-honor-claude-ai-mcp-connector-approval-required-setting), secondary, **UNVERIFIED**). Permission settings in one surface do not automatically carry over to another.

### What Claude Desk should do
1. **Claude Desk builds its own profile for each tool. The server's annotations are only the starting point.** For each tool, store a profile with effect (*looks up / changes / sends / charges / changes identity*), reach (*public / this visitor's records / any customer*), reversibility, and money cap. For curated first-party integrations (Calendar, Stripe, Drive), Claude Desk ships hand-written profiles. Custom connectors start from the annotations, are clamped to the pessimistic defaults, and need owner confirmation before their tools are enabled.
2. **Owners see sentences, not hints.** Integrations > Stripe shows *"Can look up a payment · Can send a payment link (up to $200) · Can refund. Off: refunds always need you."* Each sentence uses the three states the owner already knows from Claude: **Automatic / Ask me / Off**.
3. **Pin every tool definition.** Hash name, description, inputSchema, outputSchema and annotations at approval. On a change, the tool turns **Off** automatically and the Today queue shows a plain-English diff: *"Stripe's 'refund' tool changed what it can do. Review before Claude uses it again."* New tools on a server arrive **Off**, which follows the Managed Agents default.
4. **Keep third-party tool descriptions away from the public-facing model** where possible. For curated tools, Claude Desk writes its own descriptions. For custom tools, show the full description to the owner at approval and flag instruction-like text ("ignore", "send to", URLs).
5. **Filter the tool list by session tier.** An anonymous visitor's session receives only public tools, and verified sessions get that person's scoped tools. The spec allows tools/list to vary by authorization. A tool the model cannot see cannot be talked into.
6. **Never rely on `auto` alone for public sessions.** Visitor text counts as "intent" for an auto classifier. Anything that moves money, releases documents or changes identity is gated in code and asks the owner when above a cap.
7. **Log every gate decision** with `allow/ask/deny` and a reason code, and show it as a margin receipt in the transcript.

## 4. Owner consoles of customer-facing agents

### Intercom Fin
- **Procedures** (GA **Apr 2026**): "Combine natural language with if/else conditions and custom code". "Use code to enforce strict rules like verifying eligibility" ([fin.ai](https://fin.ai/updates/procedures-deterministic-controls)).
- **Simulations**: Fin generates test scenarios from the procedure text. Testers mock data-connector outputs and run a multi-turn chat between Fin and an **AI customer**. Intercom's guidance is to use Preview for spot checks and **Simulations "before every publish"** ([help](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/12599517-run-simulations-for-fin-procedures); [blog](https://intercom.com/blog/procedures-simulations-updates)).
- **Insights**: AI **CX Score** on every conversation with a plain-language explanation and no survey. **Topics Explorer** auto-groups conversations. **Recommendations/Suggestions** reviews conversations Fin could not resolve and proposes weekly fixes to content, data and actions, which the team **approves** ([help](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/10576273-measure-customer-service-with-insights-built-for-the-ai-agent-era)). Escalation rules are written in natural language ([Intercom ecommerce](https://www.intercom.com/solutions/ecommerce)).
- The Fin documentation reviewed describes escalation and handoff, not an approval hold on actions.
- **Reception and pricing** (G2 and a competitor blog, secondary): reviewers say "everyone raves about the Fin AI bot itself. It actually works" and that it "improves over time". Pricing is metered per resolution, at **$0.99 per resolution** ([G2](https://g2.com/products/fin-by-intercom/reviews?page=4); [Unthread](https://unthread.io/blog/intercom-review/)).

### Sierra
- **Supervisors** run in real time, in parallel with each response. They check facts, policy and tone, can redirect, and "escalate to a human". **Monitors** score **every** conversation on coherence, repetitiveness, grounding and sentiment, and teams can add **custom monitors in natural language**. A flagged conversation opens "a single view that shows the transcript, context, tone, and outcome", and from there teams "leave feedback, retrain… or create new monitors in a few clicks" ([Sierra, 29 Oct 2025](https://sierra.ai/blog/confidence-in-every-conversation)). ### Cross-vendor patterns
| Pattern | Who | Fit for Claude Desk |
|---|---|---|
| Rules as plain text, code where it must be exact | Fin Procedures, Decagon AOPs, Zendesk text procedures | Core. Show which sentence is code-enforced |

Fin and Zendesk meter pricing per resolution. All of these products target support teams, not a solo owner.

### What Claude Desk should do
1. **Rules screen = sentences** (as in Zendesk's move to text), each with a status chip: *Enforced by code / Claude's judgment / Asks you*. Plain-English procedures can expand to show their steps, but there is no flowchart editor.
2. **"Try it" simulations before go-live and after every rule change.** Generate 5–10 visitor scenarios from the rules, *including 2–3 attacks*, run them against the draft, and show pass/fail in plain words. This combines Fin Simulations with our attack ledger.
3. **Transcript with margin receipts**: every tool call, gate decision, verification and approval appears inline, citing the rule that allowed or blocked it. This is Sierra's single view and Decagon's trace, made readable.
4. **Coach from the transcript, safely**: "Teach Claude" on any message, with a pre-filled situation. Coaching that only changes *wording or knowledge* applies after a preview. Coaching that would *widen what Claude may do* becomes a rule change with a diff and the owner's explicit confirmation. Adopt Ada's "Should this have applied here?" check.
5. **Today shows why Claude needed you**, grouped by rule (Decagon Escalation Drivers), and makes weekly suggestions ("3 people asked about weekend rates, add an answer?") that the owner approves (Fin Recommendations).
6. **Predictable pricing copy**: if outcomes are metered, count them visibly on Today so the owner sees usage before the bill arrives.

## Changes to consider for Claude Desk

1. **[setup] Lead with "here's what I already understand".** After the owner allows it, read memories and the website, then show an editable business card before any questions. Connect only the integration the first use case needs (as Muse does).
2. **[rules] One promise sentence plus two global modes.** *"Claude only does what your rules allow. Anything else waits for you."* Modes: *Ask me when it commits the business* (default) / *Ask me for everything*. Locked always-ask items (payments above cap, identity changes, documents to people not on record) are marked with a lock.
3. **[rules] A status chip on every rule** (*Enforced by code / Claude's judgment / Asks you*), with rules written as sentences, never flowcharts (as in Zendesk's Oct 2026 move to text).
4. **[Today] Scoped approval ladder on every approval card**: *Allow once · Always for this customer · Always for requests like this up to $X (expires in 90 days) · Deny · Deny and block*, plus "See details" and an irreversibility note. Widening only ever happens through an owner tap (unlike Muse's "learns which decisions need approval").
5. **[verification] Codes go only to contacts on record, shown masked, and the visitor chooses among them.** A contact typed in the chat becomes a lead note, never a target. Sending only to existing contacts also removes most of the SMS-pumping risk. This is the Meta 20,225-account lesson.
6. **[verification] Inline verification card that keeps the chat alive**: one autofill/paste field, digits shown, 6 digits, 10 min, single use, 10 attempts, at most 3 sends, voice/email alternative, `aria-live` announcements. The original request resumes on success with a "Verified · ••42" receipt. Default to a code, not a magic link, because scanners consume link tokens.
7. **[verification] No record or failure means a neutral handoff**: "I can't open account details here; I'll have Turner call you", with lead capture and full context to the owner. Never confirm whether a record exists.
8. **[safety] Notify the contact on record after every verified release or account change**, with a "Not you?" path (the NIST §4.2.5 analogue). The Safety screen lists every entry point (chat, embed, agent API, takeover) running through the same verification service.
9. **[integrations] Per-tool profiles in sentences with Automatic / Ask me / Off.** Profiles are hand-written for curated integrations. For custom ones they are derived from annotations but clamped pessimistic, because annotations are untrusted. New tools arrive Off.
10. **[integrations] Pin tool definitions.** On any change to a tool's description, schema or annotations, the tool switches Off and a plain-English diff appears in the Today queue for re-approval (rug-pull defense).
11. **[conversations] Margin receipts plus "Teach Claude".** Every gate decision, tool call and verification appears inline with the rule it cited. Coaching applies to wording after a preview, while anything that widens permissions becomes an explicit rule change.
12. **[setup] "Try it as a stranger" simulations before go-live and after rule edits.** Scenarios are generated from the rules and include attack cases, with results in plain words. Today then shows "Why Claude needed you this week", grouped by rule, plus weekly suggestions to approve.
