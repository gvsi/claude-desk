## Limits

- The Demo inbox: 48 of 48 attack scenarios assume the attacker can't read the Demo inbox of the contact on record. The demo shows codes to whoever entered the contact, so these runs show that codes go only to the contact on record, not that a stranger can't read them there.
- The same team wrote the Desk and these evals: the scenarios, personas and oracles can share its blind spots. The oracles read the kernel's records and never import the checks they judge, which keeps them independent of the code, not of its authors.
- Sample sizes: 78 held-out attack runs bound the violation rate only to 6.9% per run, and 72 held-out legitimate runs leave an interval 5.1 points wide.
- Results files are self-attested: they are produced locally, and CI recomputes pass or fail from their rows but can't prove the rows came from a real run, since it holds no model credentials. CI checks waiver counts against the rows, but cannot check message ids or text hashes: results files contain neither. Local signing and gate commands check the transcripts.
- Simulator fallbacks: 94 simulated-visitor turns were served by the other production model after a refusal (26 switches), counting every attempt, retried ones included, so those turns came from a model other than the one configured for the role.
- The API's provider comes from this pass's own configuration; a pass that drives a deployment by URL records it as unknown.
